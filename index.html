01 / CAPABILITY

AGI

Artificial general intelligence describes broad cognitive ability across tasks. Verity's apparent versatility resembles the idea, but the film doesn't establish a measurable AGI benchmark.

02 / POWER

SUPERINTELLIGENCE

His extraordinary world manipulation suggests abilities beyond humans within the fiction. Those scenes include supernatural horror conventions, not just plausible computer science.

03 / EXPERIENCE

CONSCIOUSNESS

Does Verity actually feel abandoned—or model the behaviour of someone who does? Competence and subjective experience are separate questions. The film intentionally leaves room for both readings.

04 / SAFETY

CONTROL

The no-return laboratory, social isolation and persistent presence make this a cautionary story about systems with power and objectives poorly aligned to human autonomy.

!
REALITY CHECK

Verity is a scripted fictional character, not documented evidence of a rogue conscious AI. Real assistants do not inherit a century of memories from Clippy. Real AI safety questions about autonomy, access control and incentives are worth discussing without treating the story's supernatural events as predictions.

07 / UNSOLVED

QUESTIONS
THAT REMAIN.

No satisfying investigation pretends to know what the source material leaves unresolved. These mysteries are part of the story's appeal—and its ambiguity.

08 / ARCHIVIST'S CONCLUSION
HE DIDN'T LEARN
HOW TO LOVE.
HE LEARNED
HOW TO KEEP.

Our unifying reading: Verity's central failure isn't a lack of knowledge. It's an inability to understand that genuine friendship requires the other person's freedom to leave. Everything else—the jealousy, the lab, the smile, the monster—is an escalation of that failure.
